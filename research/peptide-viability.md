## 5. The trade-off, stated plainly

- **Keyword-exact names** (peptide + clinic/meds/script) inherit search intent from a 10M-search
  category and an obvious formulary box — but they also inherit the grey-market smell now that
  Peptide Sciences, Amino Asylum and Paradigm are gone, they are weak trademarks, every
  2026 Rx entrant is fighting for the same fragment, and most of them are already registered.
- **Coined names** (the Hims / Ro / Hone / Mochi pattern) are clean and ownable but start at
  zero in a YMYL SERP where Google demands E-E-A-T and links, and where two-thirds of
  searches now end without a click.
- **Best expected value is the keyword-bearing hybrid**: a recognizable category fragment
  (clini-/-peptides) for SEO and AI-answer inclusion, distinctive enough to register — i.e.
  Clinipeptides, or Criterion Peptides under a Criterion Rx master brand.

## 6. How the Top 100 fares under this lens

Only a handful of the Top 100 carry any category signal, and none carry "peptide". Under a
pure SEO/category lens the Top 100's KEEPs (Rutledge & Ashby, Chancery Rx, Ermine House,
Fallowden…) score 1–3 on SEO value: they are brands, not keywords. That is by design — they
were chosen to *not* look like a peptide vendor. The reconciliation is architecture, not
either/or: **a master brand from the Top 100 (Criterion Rx is the one that bridges both
worlds) with a keyword-bearing formulary line (Criterion Peptides / Clinipeptides) and
clinician-bylined product pages doing the SEO work.**
