The constraint that shapes every name below. Synthetic peptides such as BPC-157 and TB-500 are not lawful dietary-supplement ingredients — DSHEA excludes synthetic molecules developed as drugs, and the FDA has said so repeatedly. April 2026 removed BPC-157 from Category 2 and a July 2026 advisory committee voted 8–6 to recommend it for the 503A bulks list, but that is a compounding pathway, not a supplement pathway. A brand that sells peptides as supplements under a famous cofounder is the most enforceable target in the category. Practically, that leaves three lanes: (1) lawful peptide-adjacent supplements — amino acids, collagen peptides, creatine; (2) prescription compounded peptides via a LegitScript-certified telehealth; (3) research-use-only, which is where the celebrity makes you a target rather than a brand. The Top 100 is sorted so that lane 1 and lane 2 names are separable, and so no name traps you in lane 3.

The single most useful finding in this table: Amino is the only category noun on the list that is unambiguously lawful on a supplement label. Formula and Protocol describe a blend or a programme rather than a molecule. Peptide carries the search volume and the legal exposure in equal measure — which is why it belongs on a product line, not necessarily on the master brand.

8 · Method and limits

read before spending money
How availability was established. 17,026 candidate domains were resolved at DNS in the 2 September 2026 sweep; NXDOMAIN was treated as an open lead. 4,751 came back open on .com. This session could not re-resolve them — outbound DNS, RDAP and WHOIS are blocked by the network policy in this environment — so every domain in this document carries a 2 September date and must be re-checked at a registrar before you rely on it. Three names from the August sweep (peptura, peptorra, peptidyne) were registered by others between sweeps, so the drift is real: assume a few percent of the list has moved.
What this document is not. It is a naming and collision screen, not legal clearance. Before committing to any name: run a USPTO knockout search in Class 5 (supplements) and Class 44 (telehealth), check the social handles, and have counsel confirm which of the three lanes above the product actually sits in.
