# The a16z Verdict — What Brand & Domain Would I Actually Invest In?

**Framed as:** a Managing Director evaluating a peptide/longevity company at seed/Series A.

## The uncomfortable truth this scan surfaced

I ran three fresh scans through the investor lens — iconic single words, elegant coinages, and `[word]stack.com`. The result was decisive:

- **Iconic single-word `.com`s: 181 of 186 taken.** Aevum, Vivos, Elysia, Renova, Lumina, Vitera, Fount, Vireo — all gone.
- **Elegant coinages: 83 of 92 taken.** The only "available" ones were typo artifacts.
- **`[word]Stack.com`: every strong one taken** — including your own examples **LifeStack, BioStack, AlphaStack**, plus Vital/Prime/Peak/Health/Gene/Power/Iron/Cell/Nova/Core/Pure-Stack.

**Conclusion:** at hand-register prices, an *iconic* brand `.com` does not exist. This is the single most important finding of the whole project.

## How I actually evaluate a brand as an investor

The brands that won this category prove one pattern: **none put the molecule in the name.**
- **Function Health** (labs) — benefit, not mechanism; ~$2.5B.
- **Superpower** (labs) — aspirational platform.
- **Levels** (glucose) — one clean word.
- **Oura** (ring), **Ro**, **Hims** — short/coined, expanded far beyond the first product.

An investable brand is: **(1) not category-locked** (peptides are the *wedge*, not the identity), **(2) memorable in one hearing, (3) credible not grey-market, (4) an exact-match one-word `.com`, (5) timeless in a logo.**

By that bar, **no `[word]peptide.com` should ever be the company brand** — Alder included. It was topping my list only because I was optimizing trademark-whitespace, not brand equity. That was the wrong lens. Corrected.

## What I would invest in

**My recommendation is a strategy, not a leftover domain:**

### 1. The name must NOT contain "peptide."
Peptides are the launch product; the brand should be a **longevity / human-optimization platform** so it survives the science evolving (GLP-1s, gene therapy, whatever's next). Name for the *outcome*.

### 2. Treat the domain as an acquisition, not a registration.
The $15k ceiling buys a *decent* aftermarket name but not an elite one-word `.com` (those run **$30k–$500k**). For a venture-backed brand this is the cheapest equity you'll ever buy — **budget $50–150k and acquire the exact-match `.com`.** Prime acquisition targets (all taken, all brokerable): **fount.com, vireo.com, aevum.com, elysian.com, fountain.com, renew.com, thrive.com.**

### 3. If self-funding under $15k, the one available name I'd actually build on:

> ## 🏆 **Vitanova** — `vitanova.com` (available lead)
> **"New life"** (Latin). It's the only name in the entire 4,900-candidate study that reads like a **real consumer-health brand** instead of a supplement SKU: two-beat, benefit-oriented, no "peptide," internationally legible, logo-able, and platform-able (a longevity company, not a peptide reseller). Grab `vitanova.ai` as the tech-halo backup.
> *Caveat: "Vita Nova" is a common phrase — run a USPTO knockout (Class 5/44); there may be regional food/wine uses to clear. But as a hand-registerable brand, it's the class of the field.*

**Runners-up (available leads), ranked by investability:**
1. **Peptovance** (`peptovance.com`) — coined, ownable, collision-clean; only knock is the "pep" tell.
2. **Spancell** (`spancell.com`) — "lifespan cell," short, biohacking-legible.
3. **Reeflongevity** (`reeflongevity.com`) — evocative, but compound and longer.

### The honest MD bottom line
If this were my investment, I would **not** let the founder ship a `[word]peptide.com`. I'd fund a **domain-acquisition line item** and buy a one-word `.com`, because in DTC health the brand *is* the moat and underspending on it is the classic seed-stage mistake. If capital is truly constrained, **Vitanova** is the one name here I'd greenlight without wincing.
