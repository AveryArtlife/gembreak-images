## Strategy in one paragraph

"Leaving art to heirs" is a YMYL (your-money-your-life) topic sitting at the intersection of the ~$1T generational art transfer and near-zero authoritative content — the current answers are scattered across law-firm blogs, auction-house PDFs, and Reddit. It has **two audiences with opposite emotions and one shared destination**: boomer collectors planning (anxious, legacy-minded, 3–10 years from a transaction) and heirs who just received art (overwhelmed, transaction-ready **now**). Cluster A builds trust and captures the collector *before* the estate event; Cluster B converts heirs directly into the /sell consignment funnel; Cluster C manufactures the citations that make AI engines (and journalists) treat ArtLife as *the* source. Because ~90% of AI-engine citations are third-party, original data and tools matter more than volume of posts.

**Trust requirements (non-negotiable for YMYL):** every tax/legal article carries a named author, a reviewing trust-and-estates attorney or CPA credited with bio ("Reviewed by…"), a dated "current as of" line (tax numbers change), schema markup (Article + FAQPage + Person), and a "this is not tax or legal advice" disclaimer. One error in a tax article can undo the whole moat.

**Key facts the cluster is built on (verify at publish time):**
- Federal estate tax exemption: **$15M/person, $30M/couple, permanent from Jan 1, 2026 (OBBBA)**, indexed; 40% rate above it; annual gift exclusion $19K.
- **Step-up in basis** at death wipes out built-in capital gains — the single most misunderstood and most valuable fact for heirs.
- Sold collectibles face a **28% federal capital-gains rate** (plus 3.8% NIIT) — higher than stocks; another reason timing matters.
- Estates must obtain qualified appraisals for artworks (Treas. Reg. §20.2031-6(b)); the **IRS Art Advisory Panel** reviews works ≥$50K.
- 12 states + DC levy their own estate tax; **Florida has none** — a Miami gallery's home-field advantage.
