01

What GemBreak is

GemBreak sells sealed watch packs. Open one and there is a real wristwatch inside — always. Sometimes it's an everyday icon. On a great day, it's a grail.

Your job as a creator is simple: capture the moment the seal comes off. The suspense, the reveal, the reaction, the wrist shot. That moment is the entire product — we are an entertainment and horology brand, not a numbers game.

Two sentences to internalize before you shoot anything:

The promise

"Every pack holds a real watch."

No empty packs, no digital junk, no consolation stickers. This is the single most important true thing about GemBreak — say it often, and never promise anything beyond it.

The energy

"It's an unboxing, not a wager."

We film the joy of opening something, the same way sneaker and trading-card culture does. The moment content starts feeling like a casino, we've lost the plot — and probably the ad account.

The range, from everyday icons to grails. Show the watch you actually opened — never present the rarest pieces as the expected outcome.

02

Creative direction

Shoot it like a moment you'd want to remember, not an ad you were paid to make.

Look

Natural, tactile, close

Real rooms, real hands, real light. Get macro-close on the seal, the foil, the dial catching light. The watch photography world is dark and dramatic — your videos should feel warm and human by contrast.

Sound

Let the pack talk

The tear of the seal, the click of a clasp, an honest gasp. Clean original audio beats trending sounds. Never use casino sound effects — no coin showers, no jackpot bells, no cash-register dings.

Pace

Hook in 2 seconds

Open on the pack in-hand or mid-tear, never on an intro. Hold the reveal until at least the midpoint. End on the wrist shot — the watch being worn is the payoff, not a price.

Faces

Reaction is the content

Frame so we see hands AND face. A quiet "no way" from a real person outperforms staged screaming every time. If it didn't happen, don't fake it.

Wear it

Always end on-wrist

Whatever comes out of the pack goes on a wrist before the video ends. It reframes the whole video around the object and the person — style, fit, feel — not around value.

Honesty

Real packs, real outcomes

Only film genuine sealed packs and show what actually came out. No planted reveals, no re-seals, no borrowing a grail for the camera. One faked video is an FTC problem and a brand-ending clip.

03

Content ideas

Twelve formats that work. Steal them as-is or remix them — just run everything through the Red Zone (chapter 05) before you post.

Format 01 · The flagship

Strangers open packs

  • 0–2s"I'm letting total strangers open these…" — pack in hand, street scene behind.
  • MidHand the pack over. Film the tear, the pause, the reaction. Let them talk.
  • EndThey try it on. Ask one question: "Keeping it?"

Why it works: unscripted reactions from real people are the most trusted content on the internet. Adults only, signed release, never outside casinos, schools, or bars.

Format 02

One take, no cuts

  • 0–2s"No edits. Whatever is in this pack, you see it when I do."
  • MidSingle continuous shot, tear to reveal. Silence is allowed.
  • EndHonest verdict, on-wrist, even if it's a humble piece.

Why it works: the no-cut promise kills the "it's staged" comment before it's typed.

Format 03

The handoff

  • 0–2s"My dad has worn the same watch for 30 years. Today that changes."
  • MidGift the pack. Film them opening it — their pace, their hands.
  • EndPut it on their wrist for them. Hold the hug.

Why it works: gifting reframes the pack as an emotional object. Highest share-rate format we've seen.

Format 04

Wear whatever comes out

  • 0–2s"I'm wearing whatever is inside this pack for the next 7 days."
  • MidReveal, first impressions, day-1 fit.
  • EndTease the series: "Day 2 tomorrow. It's growing on me."

Why it works: turns one pack into a week of episodes and shows the watch living on a wrist.

Format 05

ASMR break

  • 0–2sMacro shot, nail under the seal, no talking.
  • MidEvery sound crisp: foil, tissue, clasp, bezel.
  • EndDial fills the frame. One caption line, nothing else.

Why it works: pure sensory satisfaction — the format most likely to loop.

Format 06

Watch nerd explains

  • 0–2s"You just watched me unbox this. Here's what it actually is."
  • Mid60 seconds of real horology: the movement, the history, why collectors care.
  • End"Now you know more than the guy at the party wearing one."

Why it works: education content earns saves and positions GemBreak inside watch culture, not gambling culture.

Format 07

Style it three ways

  • 0–2s"One pack, one watch, three outfits."
  • MidQuick-cut fits: office, dinner, weekend.
  • End"Which one is it? Comments."

Why it works: pulls fashion audiences in and makes the watch the hero object.

Format 08

The group break

  • 0–2s"Brought packs to the barbershop." (or office, or locker room)
  • MidEveryone opens at once. Cross-cut the reactions.
  • EndGroup wrist-check lineup, biggest reaction gets the last word.

Why it works: communal joy, multiple reactions per video, endless location remixes. Everyone on camera is 25+.

Format 09

Grandma meets a grail

  • 0–2s"My grandma has no idea what a Submariner is. Let's fix that."
  • MidShe opens a pack, you translate her honest review.
  • EndShe keeps it. Obviously.

Why it works: cross-generation reactions are comment magnets and unmistakably wholesome.

Format 10

Rank the rotation

  • 0–2s"Ranking every watch I've unboxed this month. No mercy."
  • MidTier the pieces on style, comfort, versatility — never on value.
  • EndCrown a daily driver. Defend it in the comments.

Why it works: ranking formats farm engagement, and judging on style keeps money out of the frame.

Format 11

Setup & collection tour

  • 0–2s"Three months of GemBreak packs. This is the collection now."
  • MidWatch box tour — each piece gets five seconds and one memory.
  • End"The next slot is empty on purpose."

Why it works: collections tell a story of taste, not spending. Never total up what the collection is "worth."

Format 12

The blind trade

  • 0–2s"We each open one. Then we decide: keep yours or swap, before looking twice."
  • MidTwo creators, two reveals, one agonizing decision.
  • EndThe swap (or the refusal). Rematch teased.

Why it works: built-in tension and a second creator's audience. Frame the decision around taste — "which one suits you" — never around which is worth more.

05

The Red Zone

The non-negotiables. These rules exist to keep every account involved — yours and ours — alive.

Meta treats anything with "monetary value as part of a method of entry and prize" as gambling, and gambling content requires special written authorization that surprise-product brands don't get. Enforcement is automated: software scans captions, on-screen text, hashtags, and speech, and it flags on pattern-matches, not context. Appeals take weeks and often fail. The platforms are tightening, not loosening — Meta moved gambling authorizations into Business Suite in 2025 and banned entire ad categories in 19 markets in early 2026, and TikTok Shop banned mystery-box mechanics outright.

So our standard is deliberately stricter than the letter of any policy: if a word, sound, or shot could pattern-match to gambling, we don't use it — anywhere. Not in captions, not in overlays, not in hashtags, not spoken out loud, not in comment replies. We do not argue with robots.

!

These lists apply to every surface of every post — spoken audio, on-screen text, captions, hashtags, alt text, stickers, poll options, pinned comments, and your replies. Auto-captions transcribe what you say, so a banned word said out loud becomes banned text automatically.

Banned content styles — formats we never make

Casino aesthetics

No slot-machine graphics or filters, spinning wheels, roulette animations, scratch-to-reveal effects, coin showers, Vegas neon, playing-card motifs, or casino/jackpot sound effects. This is the fastest way to get pattern-matched.

Price math on screen

No "$150 pack → $13,400 watch" comparisons, no price-tag reveals, no calculator overlays, no totaling a collection's value, no reacting with dollar figures. If a number has a currency symbol, it doesn't go in the video.

Profit & flip framing

Never present a pack as a way to make money: no resale talk, no "better than stocks," no counting cash, no "I'm up this month." GemBreak is entertainment you spend on, like concert tickets — not an earning strategy.

Compulsion content

No "buying packs until I get the grail," no marathon-opening framed as chasing one watch, no joking about addiction, no spending-spree content, no "my wife doesn't know" bits. Openings are fun, controlled, and occasional.

Outcome promises

Never imply a grail is likely, "due," or guaranteed. Don't stitch together only grail reveals — if a big open goes viral, pin a comment noting most packs hold everyday pieces. Misleading-claims flags hit exactly like gambling flags.

Creator-run promotions

Don't run your own giveaways, contests, "buy-to-enter" anything, or discount-code games of chance. All promotions are brand-run and lawyer-approved. Your job is content, never mechanics.

Gambling-adjacent placements

No filming in or near casinos or betting shops, no duets/collabs with gambling accounts, no gambling-stream references, no alcohol during openings. Guilt by association is real to a classifier.

Anyone under 25 on camera

Everyone who appears on camera — you, friends, strangers — must be clearly adult; our internal bar is 25+, borrowed from alcohol marketing. Never hand a pack to a minor, never film kids reacting, never target or reply to under-18 audiences.

The gray zone — allowed, with care
pullCommunity slang, but it's card-break vocabulary — prefer "open" or "unbox" in any written text; keep it rare in speech.
grailFine — it's collector culture. Use it about taste and desirability, never with numbers attached.
rareFine when talking about a model's history or the watch market generally. Never about pack contents or frequencies.
random"Random strangers" — fine. "Random rewards" — banned. The word is safe about people, dangerous about outcomes.
freeOnly for genuinely free things (shipping, a gifted pack you disclose). Never "chance at a free watch."
surpriseOur preferred framing — "a surprise inside every pack." Keep it about the moment, not the value.

06

Captions, tags & disclosure

Captions get scanned harder than video. Keep them short, human, and clean.

Disclosure

#ad, first line, every time

Paid or gifted, the FTC requires clear disclosure: #ad visible in the first line before "…more", plus the platform's Paid Partnership label via the branded-content tool. Both, always.

Tags

Keep the tag set boring

@gembreak + #GemBreak + your usual lifestyle tags. Skip anything from the banned list, and skip tag-stuffing entirely — 3 to 5 tags is plenty.

Comments

Your replies count too

If someone comments "what are the odds?" or "how much is that worth?", reply with the one approved line: "Every pack holds a real watch — that's the only promise." Then move on. Never answer with numbers.
